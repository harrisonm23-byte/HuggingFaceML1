Deutsche Bank reported its highest annual pretax profit in 15 years, reaching €5.6 billion after a 65% increase. Net profit more than doubled to €5.7 billion. Revenues increased 7% to €27.2 billion while noninterest expenses declined 5% to €20.4 billion. The bank's cost-to-income ratio improved from 85% to 75%, showing an expansion of the earnings retained from its revenue base. The Investment Bank generated €10.0 billion of net revenue, and its Fixed Income and Currencies businesses produced €8.9 billion, up 26% and the highest level for a decade. Rates, emerging-markets and foreign-exchange revenues were significantly higher amid active client markets and increased trading flows. Financing income also benefited from stronger interest income. The bank's continuing businesses produced improved returns as expense discipline and market activity combined to produce strong financial performance.

Deutsche Bank achieved record full-year earnings, with pretax profit rising 84% to €9.7 billion and net profit approximately doubling to €7.1 billion. Group revenues climbed 7% to €32.1 billion. Every major operating business recorded double-digit profit growth. The Investment Bank's pretax profit increased 20% to €4.0 billion, while Corporate Bank pretax profit rose 24% to €2.6 billion. Asset Management profit grew 55%, and Private Bank profit advanced 95%. The fourth quarter also set a record for that reporting period, with pretax earnings of €2.0 billion compared with €583 million a year earlier and net profit of €1.6 billion versus €337 million. The bank reported stronger returns on tangible equity and a lower cost-to-income ratio as profits rose faster than the underlying revenue base. It also proposed increased distributions to shareholders, supported by the stronger financial performance.

Deutsche Bank reported its strongest net profit in a decade as annual net earnings increased approximately fourfold to €2.5 billion. Net revenue grew 6% to €25.4 billion, supporting improved returns and a stronger operating result across the continuing business. The bank pointed to improved cost discipline and a business structure that had become more concentrated in its core financial franchises. Profitability strengthened while management outlined plans to return additional capital to shareholders, including a proposed distribution of around €700 million. The report framed the year as a marked improvement in the bank's financial trajectory, with rising profits and greater efficiency after a multi-year transformation. Performance-based variable compensation also rose, reflecting stronger results within the bank's businesses. The central financial story was the sharp increase in net income from the prior period, revenue growth and a better earnings profile.

All three figures set company records. Return on average common shareholders' equity reached 23.0%, its highest level since 2007, while the return on tangible common equity was 24.3%. The scale of earnings reflected a highly profitable year for the firm's investment banking, securities and other client businesses. Its profit generation substantially outpaced the levels achieved in more ordinary market periods. The bank emphasized its ability to serve a broad base of corporate, institutional and investing clients through demanding and active financial markets. High revenues translated into large absolute profits and exceptionally strong returns relative to common equity. The financial report was defined by record operating results rather than one successful transaction or an isolated trading position.
